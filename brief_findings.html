Brief Findings: LLM-Discovered Results in Linear Algebra

Background. We (Cameron Musco and Christopher Musco), in collaboration with Tyler Chen and Tao He, have been running a very primitive AI-driven research system to support our work on two NSF grants: The Query Complexity of Linear Algebra (awards 2427362 and 2427363) and Fine-Grained Theory for Matrix Computations (awards 2615856 and 2615857). We are not AI power users: the system is basically a git repository of open questions and partial results that we point agents to, asking them to make progress on the questions. It has produced solutions to a handful of problems that we have been interested in for some time, and we created this page to record those results.

Content Provided. These are results that we do not plan to write standalone papers about, but seem interesting enough that we want others to know about them. For each one, we give a short description of the result and explain why we think it, or the technique used to prove it, is interesting. We also link to both an LLM-written, plain-English proof and a Lean formalization. We have not made major edits to either.

(Non-)Attribution. We are not sharing these results to claim any sort of ownership over them, as we do not feel any ownership. We just find them interesting, and want to keep people in the community from duplicating work (and tokens). If, for example, you write a simplification or better exposition of one of these results, or include one in your own paper, we ask that you do not cite or acknowledge us by name. If it would help to link to this page for context, that seems fine. If you do, consider just saying something along the lines of: “Since [month, year], modern AI reasoning models have been able to prove the following result:”
